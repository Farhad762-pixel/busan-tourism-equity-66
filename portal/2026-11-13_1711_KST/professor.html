For the professor / short guide

Understand the project in 5 minutes.

A clear orientation to the question, actual evidence, limitations and exactly what feedback is needed.

1. What is the paper about?

Can a transparent Busan attraction recommender give more simulated visibility to suitable places in districts with relatively low covered foreign-card spending compared with estimated foreign visitation activity—without a large text-relevance penalty?

2. What has been done?

  • Collected and documented 207 official tourist attractions and a private 16-district × 48-month tourism indicator panel.
  • Built a TF-IDF text baseline and economic-undercapture-aware ranking experiment.
  • Independently reran the 33-query main experiment under five policy strengths and matched the saved result table.
  • Completed a small blinded human relevance pilot with two volunteers across six tourist searches.
  • Prepared literature positioning and a complete supervisor-reviewable first manuscript.

3. The main numerical finding

At λ = 0.8, simulated target-district exposure shifted from 56.41% to 70.14% (+13.74 percentage points), retaining 99.06% TF-IDF similarity. This is an offline ranking metric, NOT a change in measured revenue or human satisfaction.

4. The biggest unresolved scientific questions

  • Does the relative visitation/card-spending signal adequately represent a meaningful district economic policy goal?
  • Is its use as an intra-city ranking signal distinguishable from prior fairness/sustainable-tourism work after full-text review?
  • What held-out and larger human evaluation would be sufficient to support a submission?
  • How should route-based travel constraints and provider data limitations be addressed?
