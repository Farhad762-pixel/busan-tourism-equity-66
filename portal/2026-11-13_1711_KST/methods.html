1. Observe the district indicators

The saved panel has 16 districts × 48 months (2022–2025). The visitor series is a telecom-derived estimated district-activity measure, which cannot be summed and read as unique city visitors. Foreign-card spending has different transaction coverage and may be revised.

2. Define a relative undercapture signal

For a district, compare its share of estimated foreign visitation activity with its share of recorded foreign-card tourism consumption. The result is a relative mismatch proxy, not proof of economic need.

Economic undercapture = max(0, 1 − spending share ÷ visitation-indicator share)

Policy calibration primarily uses 2024–2025 data; 2022–2025 supports historical descriptives. Payment availability, visit duration and transport stopovers can affect the mismatch.

3. Rank the official attraction corpus

A TF-IDF text baseline matches English visitor searches to available attraction title, subtitle, place, district, address and transport words. The 48 research-authored development searches include 33 eligible cases with at least five positive text matches. The first model ranks up to 10 positive results from its top 50 candidates.

4. Apply a transparent economic multiplier

New score = text relevance × (1 + λ × district undercapture)

Compare λ = 0, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 2.0, measuring rank-weighted district exposure, cosine-similarity retention and concentration. This is the independently reproduced offline economic experiment.

5. Diagnose weak recommendations

The six-search human pilot found relatively poor matches for “local market” and “photography walk.” Planned version 2 should use verified attraction categories and a fresh unseen evaluation benchmark.
